Upright intolerance is patient language many people use when symptoms get worse sitting or standing and ease when lying down. The tracking problem is simple to describe but hard to maintain: the pattern matters, but the details disappear if they are scattered.

## What makes an upright intolerance tracker useful

The best tracker should help you answer questions like:

- What symptoms worsen when I am upright?
- What position was I in when symptoms changed?
- What else was happening that day?
- What did the symptoms stop me from doing?
- Can I review this before an appointment?

That is why upright intolerance tracking works best when symptoms, position, context, and function stay connected.

## How this differs from orthostatic intolerance tracking

Upright intolerance and orthostatic intolerance often point to a similar lived pattern, but people search for them differently.

Orthostatic intolerance is the more formal phrase. Upright intolerance is often the plain-language version someone uses when they know standing or sitting upright makes symptoms worse.

For the more formal version, [Best Symptom Tracker for Orthostatic Intolerance](/blog/best-symptom-tracker-for-orthostatic-intolerance/) is the closest companion page. For a simple definition, start with [What Is Orthostatic Intolerance?](/blog/what-is-orthostatic-intolerance/).

## What should an upright intolerance tracker include?

The most useful tracker usually includes:

- symptom history
- position notes like sitting, standing, or lying down
- relevant heart rate or blood pressure observations when useful
- flare context
- daily-function impact
- hydration, salt, or medication context when relevant
- a way to review the pattern before appointments

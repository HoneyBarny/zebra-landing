Pre-syncope is the "I might faint" feeling that does not always become full [syncope](/knowledge/syncope/). It can be brief, strange, and hard to describe later. You may remember the fear of it more clearly than the details.

That is why tracking helps. A short note made soon after an episode can show your clinician what happened, what position you were in, what may have triggered it, how long recovery took, and how it affected your day.

This is especially useful when symptoms overlap with [dysautonomia](/knowledge/dysautonomia/), [POTS](/knowledge/pots/), or [orthostatic intolerance](/knowledge/orthostatic-intolerance/). The goal is not to diagnose yourself. The goal is to make the pattern easier to review.

## Track the warning signs first

Start with what you felt before the episode peaked.

Useful warning signs include:

- lightheadedness or dizziness
- tunnel vision, black spots, or blurry vision
- muffled hearing or ringing
- nausea
- sweating, flushing, or feeling suddenly hot
- shakiness or weakness
- palpitations or racing heart
- chest discomfort
- shortness of breath
- brain fog or confusion
- feeling like you needed to sit or lie down immediately

If you cannot write much, use a simple phrase like "vision closing in, hot, shaky, had to lie down." That is enough to preserve the episode.

## Add posture and time upright

For dysautonomia and orthostatic symptoms, position matters.

Track:

- lying, sitting, standing, walking, showering, or climbing stairs
- how long you had been upright
- whether symptoms improved after sitting
- whether symptoms only improved after lying down
- whether symptoms returned when you stood again
- whether you fully fainted or stayed conscious

The American College of Cardiology, American Heart Association, and Heart Rhythm Society describe orthostatic intolerance as symptoms that develop on standing and improve with sitting or lying. That is why a posture note can be more useful than a long symptom paragraph.

## Capture the context that may have lowered your threshold

Pre-syncope can happen when several stressors stack together.

Write down anything that applied:

- heat, hot shower, or warm room
- standing still in a line
- dehydration or lower fluid intake than usual
- missed meals
- large meal
- alcohol or caffeine if relevant
- poor sleep
- flare day
- infection, period, migraine, or pain spike
- travel, errands, stairs, or exertion
- medication start, stop, dose change, or missed dose
- emotional stress or medical procedure

You are not trying to prove one cause. You are collecting context so repeated episodes are easier to compare.

## Track recovery time

Recovery is often the missing detail.

After the episode, note:

- how long it took to feel safe sitting up
- how long before you could stand again
- whether you needed to lie flat
- whether you needed fluids, food, salt, cooling, or help
- whether fatigue or brain fog lasted minutes, hours, or the rest of the day
- whether you had to cancel work, school, caregiving, driving, cooking, or plans

"Recovered after 10 minutes" and "lost the rest of the day" are very different clinical stories.

## Add vitals only when they are safe and relevant

If your clinician has asked you to monitor heart rate or blood pressure, pair numbers with position and timing.

For example:

- lying heart rate and blood pressure
- standing heart rate and blood pressure
- time since standing
- symptoms at the same moment
- what happened after sitting or lying down

Do not chase numbers during an unsafe episode. If you feel like you may faint, your first job is to get safe. Vitals are helpful only when collecting them does not put you at risk.

## Note safety details and red flags

Some episodes need medical attention. Ask your clinician what should count as urgent for you.

In your tracker, mark:

- whether you fell
- whether you were injured
- whether you lost consciousness
- whether someone witnessed the episode
- whether you had chest pain, trouble breathing, new weakness, severe headache, or symptoms that felt unusual for you
- whether emergency care was needed

MedlinePlus notes that fainting can sometimes signal a serious problem, so new, severe, or unexplained episodes are worth discussing with a healthcare professional.

## A quick pre-syncope note template

Use this when your brain is foggy:

1. Position: standing, sitting, shower, stairs, walking, line.
2. Warning signs: vision, nausea, heat, weakness, palpitations, dizziness.
3. Context: heat, hydration, food, sleep, pain, meds, exertion.
4. Action: sat down, lay down, fluids, salt, cooling, help.
5. Recovery: minutes, hours, rest of day.
6. Impact: what you had to stop or cancel.

The best tracker is the one you can use while tired. Short and consistent beats perfect and forgotten.

## Key takeaways

- Track pre-syncope even when you do not fully faint.
- Record warning signs, posture, time upright, context, recovery, and daily impact.
- Add vitals only when your clinician wants them and it is safe to record them.
- Mark falls, injuries, loss of consciousness, and unusual symptoms clearly.
- Short notes made soon after the episode are usually more useful than long notes written from memory.

## FAQ

### What is pre-syncope?

Pre-syncope means feeling like you may faint without fully losing consciousness. It can include lightheadedness, weakness, nausea, sweating, vision changes, and feeling unsteady.

### What should I track during a pre-syncope episode?

Track warning signs, posture, time upright, activity, triggers, recovery time, vitals if available, medications, hydration, salt, and what you had to stop doing.

### Should I track episodes that do not end in fainting?

Yes. Near-fainting episodes can still show a pattern, especially when they repeat with standing, heat, meals, showers, exertion, dehydration, or medication changes.

### When should I seek medical help for pre-syncope?

Ask your clinician which symptoms are urgent for you. Seek urgent care for concerning symptoms such as chest pain, trouble breathing, injury, new neurologic symptoms, or episodes that feel different or severe.

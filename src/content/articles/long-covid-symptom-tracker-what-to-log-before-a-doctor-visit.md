[Long COVID](/knowledge/long-covid/) can involve symptoms that come and go, change over time, and affect multiple parts of daily life. A useful Long COVID symptom tracker should not ask you to write a perfect diary. It should help you preserve the parts that are hardest to reconstruct before a doctor visit: fatigue, brain fog, delayed worsening, dizziness on standing, breathlessness, palpitations, sleep disruption, pain, medications, activity, and function.

The goal is not to prove every cause. The goal is to make your history easier to review with a clinician.

## Start with symptom domains

Long COVID can include many symptoms, so a body-wide checklist can become overwhelming. Start with domains instead.

Useful domains include:

- fatigue or low energy
- [brain fog](/knowledge/brain-fog/), memory, or concentration changes
- [PEM](/knowledge/pem/) or post-exertional symptom worsening
- dizziness, lightheadedness, or symptoms when upright
- fast heart rate or palpitations
- breathlessness, cough, or chest discomfort
- sleep disruption
- headache
- pain in muscles, joints, or chest
- digestive symptoms
- smell or taste changes
- mood, stress, or isolation context

On a hard day, choose the top one to three domains. That is often enough.

## Track delayed worsening without overtracking

Many people with Long COVID describe symptoms that worsen after physical, cognitive, emotional, or social effort. CDC and NHS resources describe post-exertional malaise or post-exertional symptom exacerbation as a common part of the Long COVID picture for some people.

If this applies to you, track:

- what the activity or stressor was
- when it happened
- when symptoms worsened
- which symptoms changed
- how long recovery took
- what you could not do afterward

Example:

| Day | Context | Later change | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Grocery trip and phone call | Worse fatigue and brain fog Tuesday | Needed bed rest, missed work block |
| Thursday | Shower and paperwork | Dizziness and headache that evening | Could not cook dinner |

This helps you show the pattern without writing a long essay every day.

## Record upright symptoms separately

Long COVID can overlap with [orthostatic intolerance](/knowledge/orthostatic-intolerance/) symptoms such as dizziness when standing, lightheadedness, fast heart rate, or feeling worse upright.

When that pattern appears, record:

- posture: lying, sitting, standing, after showering, after standing in line
- time upright before symptoms
- heart rate or blood pressure if you safely track them and your clinician wants that context
- symptoms at that moment
- whether sitting helped
- whether lying down helped
- recovery time

This does not diagnose POTS, dysautonomia, or orthostatic hypotension. It gives your clinician a clearer description of what happens when you are upright.

## Keep activity and function together

For Long COVID, symptoms matter. Function often explains the burden.

Add examples like:

- needed to lie down after showering
- could not finish a work block
- could not read, drive, cook, or clean
- had to cancel plans
- needed help with errands or childcare
- could not repeat an activity the next day
- needed more breaks than before

This helps the appointment focus on what the symptoms are doing to your life, not just symptom names.

## Track medications, tests, and care notes in the same timeline

Long COVID care can involve multiple visits, tests, medications, supplements, referrals, and self-management changes.

Track:

- new medications
- dose or timing changes
- missed doses, if relevant
- supplements if your clinician tracks them with you
- inhalers, pain medicines, sleep medicines, or as-needed medicines
- tests and results you want to ask about
- referrals or follow-up plans
- questions that came up after the visit

Do not change treatment based on a tracker alone. Use the record to make the next visit clearer.

## A low-effort Long COVID note template

Use this when energy is limited:

1. What changed most today?
2. What happened nearby: activity, illness, stress, sleep, meal, heat, medication?
3. Did symptoms worsen later?
4. What did it stop you from doing?
5. What question should you bring to the next visit?

If you only answer one question, answer the fourth. Functional impact is often the part memory loses first.

## What to summarize before the visit

Before a doctor visit, pull out:

- top 3 symptom domains
- biggest change since the last visit
- delayed crashes or PEM/PESE pattern
- upright symptoms or palpitations, if present
- breathlessness or chest symptoms, if present
- sleep changes
- medication or supplement changes
- examples of functional impact
- top 3 questions

Helpful questions include:

- "Which symptoms should we prioritize first?"
- "Could any symptoms suggest dysautonomia, POTS, ME/CFS-like PEM, breathing issues, cardiac issues, anemia, thyroid issues, medication effects, or something else to evaluate?"
- "What should I track between now and the next visit?"
- "Which symptoms should prompt urgent care?"
- "What activity limits or pacing guidance should I follow?"

## Key takeaways

- Track Long COVID by symptom domain, not by trying to log everything.
- Keep activity, delayed worsening, recovery, and functional impact together.
- Record upright symptoms separately if standing makes symptoms worse.
- Medication and care notes are easier to review when they live beside symptoms.
- The best tracker output is a short appointment summary and better questions.

## FAQ

### What should I track for Long COVID?

Track the main symptom domains, delayed worsening after activity, dizziness or fast heart rate when upright, breathlessness, sleep, medications, functional impact, and questions for your clinician.

### Should I track activity if I have PEM or PESE?

Yes, but keep it low-effort. Track the activity or stressor, when symptoms worsened, what changed, and how long recovery took.

### Can a symptom tracker diagnose Long COVID?

No. Tracking can organize your history for a healthcare visit, but Long COVID diagnosis and treatment decisions require clinical evaluation.

### How can I track without making fatigue worse?

Use the minimum useful entry: what changed most, what happened nearby, what it stopped you from doing, and one question for your appointment.

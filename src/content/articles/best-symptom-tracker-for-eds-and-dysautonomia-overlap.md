## What makes an overlap tracker actually useful

The best symptom tracker should help you answer questions like:

- Which symptoms tend to show up together?
- What seems position-related?
- What changed during the flare?
- What did the symptoms do to daily life?
- Can I explain the pattern before the appointment starts?

That is why overlap tracking needs more than raw symptom capture. It needs a structure that keeps the whole pattern reviewable.

## Generic trackers and notes

Broader trackers or notes can still work if:

- you mainly need temporary capture
- you do not mind more setup
- you already have a review system that works for you

The tradeoff is that overlap history often depends on the connection between:

- pain
- dizziness or lightheadedness
- fatigue
- brain fog
- position changes
- what changed in daily function

That is where symptom blur becomes much harder to review in a generic setup.

## What should an EDS and dysautonomia overlap tracker include?

The most useful tracker usually includes:

- symptom history
- flare patterns
- position-related context
- daily-function impact
- medication notes
- measurements only when useful
- a way to review what changed before appointments

## Should you separate EDS symptoms from dysautonomia symptoms?

Usually no.

If the symptoms are part of the same lived pattern, separating them can make the picture harder to review. A connected timeline is usually more useful than isolated category notes.

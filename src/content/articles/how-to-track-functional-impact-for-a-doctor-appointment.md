Functional impact is the part of symptom tracking that explains what symptoms changed in real life. Instead of only writing down pain, fatigue, dizziness, or brain fog, track what those symptoms stopped you from doing, changed, delayed, or made harder.

This is especially useful before a doctor appointment because symptoms can sound vague when they are listed by name alone. [Functional impact](/knowledge/functional-impact/) turns the symptom into a clearer example.

## What functional impact means

Functional impact means the practical effect of symptoms on daily life.

Examples:

- could not shower without resting afterward
- needed to lie down after standing
- missed work, school, or plans
- could not cook, clean, commute, or drive
- had trouble following a conversation
- needed help with basic tasks
- recovered more slowly than usual

These examples help show severity without needing dramatic language.

## Why symptoms alone are not always enough

A symptom list might say:

- fatigue
- dizziness
- pain
- brain fog

That list is useful, but it does not explain what changed.

A stronger appointment note says:

- fatigue made it hard to shower and cook on three days this week
- dizziness showed up after standing and forced me to sit down
- brain fog made it hard to finish work messages
- pain changed how long I could walk or sit upright

That is easier to discuss.

## What to track before a doctor appointment

Use a simple structure:

1. Symptom
2. What it affected
3. How often it happened
4. What changed recently
5. What question you want to ask

You do not need a long diary. You need a few examples that describe the real effect.

## Which daily activities to watch

Helpful categories include:

- standing
- walking
- showering
- cooking
- work or school
- driving or commuting
- sleep and recovery
- concentration
- social plans
- household tasks

Choose the categories that fit your life and your appointment.

## How to keep it sustainable

Track the days when function changed most clearly.

Good moments to record:

- flare days
- days you had to cancel something
- days a symptom changed your routine
- days you needed more help or rest
- days recovery took longer than usual

This keeps the record useful without turning it into another task you have to survive.

## How to turn it into an appointment summary

Before the visit, pull out:

- the top three symptoms affecting function
- the clearest examples
- what changed recently
- what you want help deciding next

That can become part of a [doctor-ready report](/knowledge/doctor-ready-report/) or a short note you review before the appointment.

## Key takeaways

- Functional impact makes symptoms more concrete.
- Track what symptoms changed, limited, delayed, or interrupted.
- A few clear examples can be more useful than a long symptom list.

## FAQ

### What is functional impact in symptom tracking?

Functional impact means what symptoms changed in daily life, such as work, school, chores, mobility, concentration, or self-care.

### Why should I track functional impact before a doctor appointment?

Functional examples make symptoms easier to understand because they show what changed, not only what you felt.

### Do I need to track functional impact every day?

No. Tracking clear changes, flare days, or repeated limits can be enough for a useful appointment summary.

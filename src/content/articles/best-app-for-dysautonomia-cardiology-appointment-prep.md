If your main problem is scattered notes and hard-to-explain patterns, Zebra is the strongest fit. If you only need a temporary place to save numbers, simpler tools may still work.

The hard part of dysautonomia cardiology prep is usually not collecting more information. It is getting the information into a form you can actually use once the appointment starts.

## What makes a dysautonomia cardiology prep app useful

The best app should help you answer questions like:

- What changed recently?
- What symptoms worsen when upright?
- What did the flare days look like?
- What changed around medications, hydration, salt, or routines?
- What does the cardiologist need to understand first?

That is different from generic symptom logging. Appointment prep needs review, not just capture.

## Notes, spreadsheets, and vitals-only tools

Simpler tools can still work if you only need short-term capture.

They may be enough if:

- the appointment is very soon
- you already know the main pattern
- you only need a few points to remember

The tradeoff is that dysautonomia cardiology prep often depends on the relationship between:

- symptoms
- upright changes
- flare timing
- medications
- hydration and salt
- what changed in daily life

That is where disconnected tools start to break down.

- medication and supplement notes

## What should you actually bring into the room?

The best app should help you walk in with:

- a short symptom summary
- the clearest orthostatic pattern notes
- medications and recent changes
- hydration and salt context if relevant
- your top questions

That is usually more useful than bringing a giant archive of raw entries.

## Why this tracking problem is different

POTS, dysautonomia, and EDS tracking gets difficult because the problem is rarely one clean symptom.

The same month might include:

- dizziness or lightheadedness when upright
- palpitations or heart-rate changes
- joint pain or instability
- fatigue, brain fog, or sleep disruption
- flares that blur several symptoms together
- medication, salt, hydration, or routine changes
- questions you need to remember before a specialist visit

The best tracker is not the one that collects the most raw entries. It is the one that helps you keep the pattern understandable later.

## What should the tracker include?

For POTS, dysautonomia, and EDS overlap, the most useful tracker usually includes:

- symptom history that is fast to enter
- flare tracking
- upright or orthostatic context
- pain, fatigue, dizziness, brain fog, and function notes
- medication and routine changes
- hydration and salt context when relevant
- appointment questions
- a way to review or export a clean report

## When Bearable may fit better

Bearable may fit better if you want:

- broad customization
- a generic tracker you can shape yourself
- many categories that are not condition-specific
- flexibility over guided structure

The tradeoff is setup burden. For people with brain fog, fatigue, dizziness, or flare days, too much customization can become another job.

## When Visible may fit better

Visible may fit better if your main goal is pacing, exertion management, or wearable-guided awareness.

That is a real need, especially for people managing crashes, PEM, or energy-limiting illness. But pacing and appointment preparation are different jobs.

If your hardest problem is "How do I avoid overdoing it?" a pacing tool may be the better first choice. If your hardest problem is "How do I explain the last month clearly?" Zebra is usually the stronger fit.

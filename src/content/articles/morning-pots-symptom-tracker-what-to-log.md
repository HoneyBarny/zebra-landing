Some people with [POTS](/knowledge/pots/), [dysautonomia](/knowledge/dysautonomia/), [orthostatic intolerance](/knowledge/orthostatic-intolerance/), Long COVID, EDS or HSD overlap, ME/CFS, migraine, or chronic illness notice that mornings are harder than the rest of the day. Getting out of bed may bring dizziness, racing heart, nausea, shakiness, weakness, brain fog, sweating, headache, or a need to sit back down.

A morning POTS symptom tracker does not diagnose POTS, dysautonomia, low blood pressure, dehydration, sleep disorders, medication effects, or any other condition. It helps you bring a clearer first-hour pattern to a clinician: what happened after waking, what changed the symptoms, and how much of the morning was affected.

## Start With Wake Time And Sleep Context

Morning notes are more useful when they include the night before.

Track:

- wake time
- bedtime
- sleep quality
- wake-ups overnight
- insomnia or restless sleep
- illness, fever, pain, migraine, or flare overnight
- period timing, if relevant
- alcohol, caffeine, or late meals
- whether you woke already symptomatic

Example: "Woke 7:20 after 5 hours broken sleep. Dizzy before standing, brain fog 6/10. Symptoms worsened after walking to bathroom."

That is easier to review than "bad morning."

## Track The First Position Change

For POTS and orthostatic intolerance, the shift from lying down to sitting or standing can matter.

Record:

- lying, sitting, standing, walking, or bathroom trip
- whether you got up quickly or slowly
- how long you sat before standing
- how long you were upright before symptoms started
- whether symptoms improved after sitting or lying down
- whether you had to return to bed
- how long recovery took

If you already track heart rate or blood pressure as part of your care plan, add timing and position: lying before getting up, sitting, standing, or after walking. Do not start intensive monitoring if it is unsafe or makes anxiety worse; ask your clinician what is useful.

## Add Fluids, Salt, Breakfast, And Medication Timing

Morning symptoms can sit inside a larger context.

Useful notes:

- water before getting out of bed
- fluids soon after waking
- electrolytes
- salt intake if your clinician recommends it
- skipped breakfast
- large breakfast
- refined carbohydrate-heavy breakfast
- nausea or low appetite
- medication time
- missed dose
- recent dose change

Do not change fluids, salt, meals, medications, or supplements based on a tracker alone. Use the pattern to ask better questions.

## Track The Symptoms That Shape The Morning

Write the symptoms that actually changed your morning, not every sensation.

Common morning notes may include:

- [dizziness](/knowledge/dizziness/)
- [lightheadedness](/knowledge/lightheadedness/)
- near-fainting or [syncope](/knowledge/syncope/)
- racing pulse or [tachycardia](/knowledge/tachycardia/)
- [palpitations](/knowledge/palpitations/)
- nausea
- shakiness
- sweating
- shortness of breath
- chest discomfort
- headache
- blurry vision
- leg heaviness
- weakness
- [brain fog](/knowledge/brain-fog/)
- [fatigue](/knowledge/fatigue/)

If symptoms are new, severe, one-sided, or different from your usual pattern, follow your clinician's guidance about urgent care.

## Record What Helped

Tracking improvement is just as useful as tracking symptoms.

Note whether you tried:

- sitting at the bedside before standing
- getting up more slowly
- fluids before standing
- breakfast or a small snack
- electrolytes
- salt if recommended
- compression garments
- cooling
- lying back down
- moving plans later
- help from another person

This can turn a vague pattern into practical appointment questions.

## Track Functional Impact

Morning symptoms matter because they change what you can do.

Record whether symptoms affected:

- getting dressed
- showering
- breakfast
- school or work start time
- commute
- driving
- childcare
- errands
- exercise or physical therapy
- appointments
- social plans

"Needed 20 minutes before work" and "missed work because I could not stay upright" are very different patterns.

## What To Summarize Before An Appointment

Before a primary care, cardiology, neurology, Long COVID, EDS, dysautonomia, or rehabilitation appointment, summarize:

- how many mornings per week are affected
- whether symptoms start before standing or after standing
- whether symptoms improve later in the day
- sleep context
- fluids, salt, breakfast, and medication timing
- heart rate or blood pressure context, if already measured
- what helped
- recovery time
- functional impact
- any fainting, chest pain, severe shortness of breath, confusion, injury, or new neurological symptoms

Useful questions:

- "Could this first-hour pattern be related to orthostatic intolerance?"
- "Should I track heart rate or blood pressure around waking?"
- "Should we review fluids, salt, breakfast, medications, or sleep?"
- "What symptoms should be urgent?"
- "Which morning changes are safe for me to try?"

## Key Takeaways

- Track wake time, sleep, first position change, fluids, salt, breakfast, medication timing, symptoms, recovery, and functional impact together.
- Morning symptoms may vary from day to day, so short consistent notes are more useful than perfect detail.
- Add heart rate or blood pressure only if it is already part of your care plan and safe.
- A first-hour symptom diary can make morning limitations easier to explain at appointments.

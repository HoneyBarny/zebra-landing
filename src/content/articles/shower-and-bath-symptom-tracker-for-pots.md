For some people with [POTS](/knowledge/pots/), [dysautonomia](/knowledge/dysautonomia/), [orthostatic intolerance](/knowledge/orthostatic-intolerance/), Long COVID, EDS or HSD overlap, migraine, or chronic illness, showering is not a simple daily task. A warm shower may bring dizziness, racing heart, nausea, weakness, sweating, shaky legs, headache, brain fog, or a need to lie down afterward.

A shower symptom tracker does not diagnose POTS, dysautonomia, heat illness, medication side effects, anxiety, or any other condition. It helps you bring a clearer daily-life pattern to a clinician: what kind of shower or bath triggered symptoms, what happened, what helped, and how long recovery took.

## Start With The Shower Context

You do not need a perfect bathroom log. Start with the details most likely to explain why one shower was easier or harder than another.

Track:

- shower, bath, hair wash, bath plus shower, or sink wash
- water temperature: cool, lukewarm, warm, hot
- bathroom temperature or steam
- shower length
- standing, sitting, or switching between both
- whether you washed hair
- whether arms were overhead
- time of day
- whether you had eaten
- hydration and salt context
- medication timing
- whether you were already flaring

Example: "Warm shower, 14 minutes, stood the whole time, washed hair. Dizziness started at minute 8. Heart racing after shower. Needed 35 minutes lying down before I could get dressed."

That kind of note is easier to review than "showers are awful."

## Separate Heat, Standing, And Movement

Showers can stack several triggers at once.

The pattern may involve:

- heat from the water
- warm room or steam
- standing still
- arm movement while washing hair
- bending, reaching, or turning
- low fluids
- skipped meal
- medication timing
- period, illness, migraine, or flare day

Try wording such as "hot water plus standing" or "hair wash plus arm movement" instead of deciding the cause too early. This keeps the note useful without turning it into a self-diagnosis.

## Track Symptoms During And After The Shower

Some symptoms appear in the shower. Others appear when you step out, dry off, get dressed, or try to start the day.

Track whether you noticed:

- [dizziness](/knowledge/dizziness/)
- [lightheadedness](/knowledge/lightheadedness/)
- near-fainting or [syncope](/knowledge/syncope/)
- racing pulse or [tachycardia](/knowledge/tachycardia/)
- [palpitations](/knowledge/palpitations/)
- nausea
- shakiness
- sweating more or less than expected
- shortness of breath
- chest discomfort
- headache or migraine symptoms
- blurry vision
- leg heaviness
- weakness
- brain fog
- fatigue

If a symptom feels new, severe, one-sided, or different from your usual pattern, follow your clinician's advice about urgent care.

## Add Heart Rate Or Blood Pressure Only If It Is Safe

Some people already track heart rate or blood pressure as part of a care plan. If that is you, the useful detail is the position and timing.

Possible notes:

- before shower, sitting
- during shower, if safe and already part of your plan
- immediately after shower
- after sitting or lying down
- after cooling
- when symptoms returned to baseline

Do not try to collect measurements in a slippery bathroom if it is unsafe. A plain note such as "felt heart racing after 10 minutes standing in warm shower" can still be useful.

## Track What Helped

The goal is not only to document bad moments. Track what made the shower more manageable.

Helpful context may include:

- cooler water
- shorter shower
- sitting or using a shower chair
- washing hair separately
- opening the door or reducing steam
- fan or ventilation
- drinking fluids before or after
- electrolytes
- salt if recommended by your clinician
- compression before or after
- resting before showering
- lying down afterward
- changing shower time

Do not change fluids, salt, medication, or compression plans based on an app alone. Use the pattern to ask better medical questions.

## Record Recovery And Functional Impact

Recovery is often the part that gets lost from memory.

Write down:

- how long you needed to sit or lie down
- whether you could get dressed right away
- whether you had to delay work, school, errands, cooking, driving, childcare, or social plans
- whether fatigue or brain fog lasted for hours
- whether symptoms returned later in the day
- whether you needed help from someone else

"Recovered in 10 minutes" and "lost the morning after showering" are different appointment details.

## What To Summarize Before A Doctor Visit

Before a primary care, cardiology, neurology, dysautonomia, Long COVID, EDS, or rehabilitation appointment, summarize:

- how often showers trigger symptoms
- whether baths are different from showers
- whether hot water is worse than lukewarm water
- whether standing time changes the pattern
- whether washing hair is a separate trigger
- hydration, salt, meal, and medication context
- heart rate or blood pressure context, if already measured
- what helped
- recovery time
- functional impact
- any fainting, chest pain, severe shortness of breath, confusion, injury, or heat illness concerns

Useful questions:

- "Could heat, standing, or hydration be part of this pattern?"
- "Should I track heart rate or blood pressure around showers?"
- "Are there safer shower strategies for my situation?"
- "What symptoms should be urgent?"
- "Could medication timing, meals, or recovery time matter here?"

## Key Takeaways

- Track water temperature, shower length, standing or sitting, symptoms, hydration, salt, recovery, and functional impact together.
- Showers can combine heat, steam, upright posture, arm movement, and low fluids.
- Do not collect bathroom measurements if it is unsafe; short written notes still count.
- A consistent shower diary can make a daily-life problem easier to explain at appointments.
